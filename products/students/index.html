Student Device FAQs

A good student laptop should have at least an Intel Core i3/i5 or Ryzen 3/5, 8GB RAM, and an SSD (even just 256GB) instead of an HDD. This covers about 90% of Malaysian coursework that includes browsing, Word/Excel/PowerPoint, Zoom classes and LMS portals. But for engineering, design or CS students, it’s recommended to go for 16GB RAM and a 512GB SSD because the software they run (AutoCAD, MATLAB, Adobe suite, coding environments, local AI/ML model training or running Jupyter notebooks with datasets) eats RAM fast and multitasking with 8GB becomes painful.

Yes, if it's from a reputable seller. Choose laptop units with at least a 6-12 month warranty and a disclosed battery health %. Avoid random Facebook/Mudah listings with no warranty or only a 7-day exchange.

Go for Windows if your course needs specific software (e.g., AutoCAD, MATLAB, SPSS, accounting/ERP tools) because most Malaysian university labs and lecturer files are Windows-based. But buying a MacBook would be a better option if you're in design, media, or CS, or just want great battery life and build quality. It is advised to check your faculty's official laptop/software list before deciding.

Yes. Apple Silicon Macs run Windows via Parallels Desktop (officially Microsoft-supported, no reboot needed) or Windows 365 in the browser. Older Intel Macs can dual-boot natively via Boot Camp. This works fine for occasional use but if 50%+ of your coursework depends on Windows applications, a native Windows laptop is a lot smoother.

8GB/256GB is the bare minimum for light Office/browsing use only. CS, design, and engineering students should go for 16 - 32GB RAM and 512GB - 1TB SSD. Don't skimp on RAM because it's usually not upgradeable later.

14-inch (1.2–1.5kg) is better if you commute or carry it between lecture halls daily. 15.6-inch suits students who mostly work from a desk/hostel room and want a bigger screen; it's also usually the cheaper option in budget laptop ranges.

A genuine, activated Windows 11 (or macOS on Mac), Microsoft 365 (often free via your university student email), a browser, Zoom/Teams/Google Meet, Windows Defender or basic antivirus, and cloud backup (OneDrive/Google Drive). Add course-specific software (SPSS, AutoCAD, MATLAB, Adobe CC) per your faculty's requirements.

Dell Latitude is known for comfortable keyboards, while HP EliteBook is known for premium chassis and battery longevity. The real deciding factors are generation/specs, battery health %, warranty length, and cosmetic grade (A/B/C), not the brand badge itself.

Expect 5-7 hours of actual use from most Windows laptops (screen brightness + Wi-Fi drain it faster than marketing numbers suggest). Apple Silicon MacBooks get much closer to their rating in the real world, often 12+ hours. For genuinely all-day use without hunting for a power outlet, an M-series MacBook or an efficient Windows ultrabook (Intel Evo/Ryzen U-series) is your best bet.
